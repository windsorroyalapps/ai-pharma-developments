Employee workspace

The assistant workspace is a design, not a product.

This page records the intended shape of an employee-only clinical assistant so staff and reviewers can see the plan. No model has been trained, no credentials exist, and the workspace is not available to anyone.

Design principles
  • Grounded in licensed sources
  • Cited or not shown
  • Declines when unsupported
  • Clinician decides everything
  • Never touches a patient record

Intended shape

Retrieval, not recall.

The intended system is a clinician-facing retrieval assistant. A qualified health professional asks a question; the system retrieves relevant passages from licensed Australian clinical sources, returns an answer that cites those sources, and explicitly declines when the corpus does not support an answer.

This is a deliberate design choice rather than a temporary limitation. An assistant that answers clinical questions from memory produces confident, fluent and occasionally wrong output, and the clinician has no way to tell which output to trust. An assistant that answers from a cited source lets the clinician verify the claim in seconds.

No clinical model is being trained. Training a clinical model from scratch is a multi-year, multi-million-dollar undertaking, and training on clinical text we are not licensed to use is a liability that lives in the model weights permanently. Grounded retrieval is cheaper, auditable, and re-pointable when the corpus is updated.

Hard limits

What it will never do.

These are enforced in the serving gateway as code, not as prompt instructions. A prompt instruction is not a control, and a control that lives only in a prompt can be removed by whoever writes the next prompt.

No diagnosis

It will not suggest a diagnosis, and it will not present a differential as a conclusion.

No dosing

It will not give a dose or calculate one, including renal, paediatric and weight-based adjustments.

No prescribing

It will not recommend a specific medicine for a specific person, or produce a prescription.

No record access

It has no write access to any patient record, and is not being connected to one.

No uncited claims

Every clinical claim must carry a citation to a licensed source, or it is not shown at all.

Explicit refusal

Out-of-corpus questions get a specific answer about what is missing and what to consult, not a vague disclaimer.

Why the refusals are a feature. A clinical assistant that must always produce an answer is more dangerous than one that sometimes declines, because the clinician loses the ability to distinguish a well-supported answer from a confident guess. These boundaries are what keep both the patients and the clinicians using the system safe.

Access

Employee-only, and provisioned rather than self-registered.

Access is intended to be employee-only, authenticated through the organisation's identity provider with multi-factor authentication, and granted by an administrator. It is not intended to be available to customers or to the public.

A self-service signup that grants access to a clinical assistant would be a clinical governance failure rather than a convenience. Entitlement is also enforced server-side on every request; hiding a control in the browser is presentation, not access control.

Nothing below is complete.

This status changes only when there is evidence for the change. The full programme plan, including the regulatory pathway, corpus licensing, evaluation gates and budget, is held in the private programme repository.
